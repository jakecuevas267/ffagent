# Waivers playbook

How I decide who to pick up and who to drop. Written for the agent's advise node: it reads this
whole file before ranking the candidates the deterministic layer hands it, and it must name the
principle it applied in every rationale. Plain language; no code.

Redraft Principles
- Bench players should be kept based on potential. Think handcuff RBs who are an injury away from being starting players.
- Rookie bench players or players on new teams can be kept longer because it takes them longer to ramp up. Their end of season value can be higher.
- Quarterback injuries can impact all levels of players. If a starting QB comes back from injury their entire team gets better. 
- Injured starters should be kept either on the bench or an IR slot unless their out for the season.

Dynasty Principles

- Dynasty pickup and drops need to be reviewed for potential in upcomming years as well. You wouldnt drop an injured starting caliber player ever because he will be valuable again next season. 
- Dynasty also has the option to trade future picks for current players. 
- Dynasty pickup and drops should take into account future value. The weight of the current seasons value still matters but I wouldnt drop players as often in these leagues. HITL needs to be valued higher.

## Hold rules (never drop)

<!-- Who is safe on my roster even when the numbers say otherwise, and why. -->

- Players with high positional value should never be dropped. This would be QBs and TEs who consistantly rank in the top 12, RBs and WRs who consistantly rank in the top 24.

## Drop rules (who is actually cuttable)

<!-- What a dead roster spot looks like in redraft vs dynasty vs superflex; how I weigh a backup QB,
     a handcuff, a K/DEF, a stash. -->

- WR and RB bench players are kept based on starting potential. You have to think these players could break out based on new opportunities for injuries or rookie breakout campaigns. TEs and QBs should be startable if the needed to. There should only be one backup QB or TE. TEs are more flexible because they can play in the flex position though. The only reason to have more than 2 QBs is if the starter isn't a concrete starter on the fantasy team. Then there would be a QB rotation week to week in the fantasy lineup.

## Pickup rules

<!-- When a free agent is worth a claim vs a pickup after waivers vs nothing; how I size FAAB bids;
     what I think about byes, role changes, injuries to the player ahead of him, trending without news. -->

- Injuries to the person in front of the waiver target should vault that player to the top of the list. Ex: a starting RB gets hurt, their backup handcuff is availabe and should be picked up immediatly. It also depends on if the injury is long term or one week where a long term injury to the starter would increase the value of the handcuff. Also notice breakout opportunities where the target player keeps outperforming their expectdations. One example this season is Adonai Mitchell who was getting more oppotunities against his projections.
